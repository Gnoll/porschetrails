Recall campaigns differ by country, and whether a particular car has had the work done depends on its history. Porsche has built millions of cars across many model lines, so no single page can list every campaign. Treat this one as a starting point and always check your own car against the official sources.

## How to check your car

- **Porsche:** the manufacturer provides a recall lookup by VIN on its national websites, and any Porsche Centre can check a chassis number against the factory system. Recall work is carried out free of charge.
- **United States:** enter the VIN at the NHTSA recall lookup, nhtsa.gov/recalls.
- **United Kingdom:** use the government's vehicle recall checker on gov.uk.
- **European Union:** the Safety Gate database lists vehicle safety recalls, and Germany's KBA publishes its own.
- **Australia:** the government's vehicle recalls website.

If you are buying, ask the seller for a dealer printout showing no outstanding campaigns.

## Notable recalls

These are examples that owners and buyers of sports models most often ask about. The list is not complete, and details differ by market.

| Models | Issue |
| --- | --- |
| 2014 model year 911 GT3 (991) | After two engine fires in early 2014, Porsche told owners to stop driving the cars and replaced the engines in all 785 delivered at that point. The cause was traced to a connecting rod fastener. |
| 2021 model year 718 Cayman GT4, 718 Spyder, Boxster and Cayman GTS 4.0 | Connecting rods that might not have been made to specification. Affected engines were replaced. In the United States this was NHTSA campaign 21V-200, covering a small number of cars. |
| Carrera GT | Porsche announced a recall in 2023 concerning suspension joints. Confirm with a dealer that the work has been done. |
| 918 Spyder | Several campaigns during and after production, covering chassis components, wiring and seat belt mountings among other items. |
| 2020 to 2024 Taycan | High-voltage battery modules that could develop a short circuit, with a risk of fire. Dealers analyse battery data and replace modules where necessary. In the United States the campaigns include NHTSA 24V-215, 24V-217, 24V-731 and 24V-732. An earlier recall updated software after reports of sudden loss of drive power. |

Other campaigns over the years have covered items such as airbags, seat belts, wheel fixings on centre-lock cars, fuel system parts and lighting software. Newer models pick up new campaigns regularly. Check the VIN.

## Known issues that are not recalls

These are the faults owners and specialists talk about. None is universal, and a well-maintained car may show none of them. The [model guides](/models/) list them by generation.

### M96 and M97 engines (986, early 987, 996, early 997 Carrera)

- **Intermediate shaft (IMS) bearing failure.** The best known Porsche fault of the modern era. It was the subject of a class action settlement in the United States covering certain 2001 to 2005 model year cars, but not a safety recall. Uprated replacement bearings are widely fitted.
- **Bore scoring.** Cylinder wall damage leading to oil consumption and eventually a rebuild. More often reported on the larger engines.
- **Rear main seal leaks, air-oil separator failure and cracked coolant tanks.**

### Mezger engines (996 and 997 Turbo, GT3, GT2)

- **Coolant pipe fittings** bonded into the engine castings can come loose. Pinning or welding is the accepted cure.

### 991 GT3 (first generation)

- **Valvetrain wear.** Porsche extended the engine warranty on these cars to cover it. Ask a dealer what applies to a particular car.

### Air-cooled 911s

- **Broken cylinder head studs** on SC and Carrera 3.2 engines.
- **Chain tensioners** on engines built before the pressure-fed type.
- **Cylinder-to-head oil leaks** on early 964 engines, **dual-mass flywheels** on early 964s, and **blocked secondary air injection ports** on the 993.

### Transaxle cars (924, 944, 968)

- **Timing and balance shaft belts.** Not a fault so much as a maintenance item that destroys the engine if neglected.

### All water-cooled cars

- **Front radiators and condensers** corrode when leaves collect in the intakes.
- **Vacuum change-over valves** fail with age on many models.
- **Coolant pump and thermostat** leaks on some later engines.

## A note on proportion

Forums concentrate bad news. Most of these cars cover very high mileages with nothing more than routine maintenance. The point of knowing the list is to ask the right questions when you buy and to spot a problem early.
