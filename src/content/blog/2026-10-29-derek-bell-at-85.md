Derek Bell turns 85 on Saturday 31 October, and Porsche marked the date this week with a [tribute in its newsroom](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/2026/history/porsche-derek-bell-85th-birthday-43597.html). It is a good moment to look back at one of the drivers who did most to build the company's endurance racing record.

## From club racing to the 917

Bell was born in Pinner, Middlesex, in 1941. He came up through British club racing, then Formula 3 and Formula 2, the usual route for an ambitious driver of the period. His first Le Mans in a Porsche came in 1971, at the wheel of a long-tail 917 LH, the low-drag version built for the Mulsanne straight.

That was the start of a relationship that would last for most of the next two decades. Bell was never the flashiest driver in the field. His strength was the kind of steady, mechanically sympathetic pace that wins 24-hour races: quick enough to stay in contention, careful enough to bring the car home.

## Four Le Mans wins in Porsches

Bell won Le Mans five times, which makes him Britain's most successful driver at the race. Four of those wins came with Porsche:

- **1981**, in the 936, sharing with Jacky Ickx.
- **1982**, in the new 956, again with Ickx.
- **1986** and **1987**, in the 962, with Hans-Joachim Stuck and Al Holbert.

The partnership with Ickx in the Rothmans-liveried 956 is the image most people hold of Bell, and it is the car in the photograph above. The 956 and its successor, the 962, dominated sports car racing through the mid-1980s, and Bell was at the centre of that run.

## World titles and Daytona

Le Mans was not the whole story. Bell was World Endurance Champion in 1985 and 1986, a reward for consistency across a full season rather than a single race. In America he won the Daytona 24 Hours three times in the 962, in 1986, 1987 and 1989.

In 1992 he returned to Le Mans in a 962 C with his son Justin and Tiff Needell, finishing twelfth. A father and son sharing a car at Le Mans is rare, and it said something about how long Bell had been part of the race.

## Where to see the cars today

Bell now lives in Florida and still appears at Porsche historic events and reunions. The cars he raced turn up regularly at historic meetings, and our [events calendar](/events/) lists the ones where you are most likely to see a 917, 956 or 962 run, including Le Mans Classic and Rennsport Reunion.
