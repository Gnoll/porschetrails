Fifty-two years ago this month, on 3 October 1974, Porsche put the production 911 Turbo on its stand at the Paris Motor Show. Every turbocharged 911 since traces back to that car.

## From the racetrack

Porsche learned turbocharging in racing. The 917/10 won the Can-Am championship in North America in 1972, and the 917/30 that followed in 1973 was so dominant in Mark Donohue's hands that the rules were rewritten. In 1974 the works applied the same thinking to the 911: a Carrera RSR Turbo 2.1 finished second overall at Le Mans, among purpose-built prototypes.

A road car was already on the way. A silver 911 Turbo concept had appeared at the Frankfurt show on 13 September 1973, with the word Turbo written across its rear wings.

## Number one

The first road-going 911 Turbo was not for sale. On 29 August 1974 Ferry Porsche gave it to his sister, Louise Piëch, for her 70th birthday. According to Porsche it had a turbocharged test version of the 2.7-litre engine in a narrow body, and the badge on the tail read Carrera, not Turbo.

## The Paris car

Five weeks later came the real thing. The Paris show car had a 3.0-litre flat-six with a single turbocharger and 260 PS, wide rear arches and a large rear wing. Inside it was fully trimmed, with leather and tartan seat centres. This was a deliberate choice: the Turbo was to be the flagship of the 911 range, a fast and comfortable road car, not a stripped homologation special.

Sales began in spring 1975. Inside the company the car was the Type 930, and Porsche describes it as the world's first series-production sports car with an exhaust-gas-controlled turbocharger. It sold better than Porsche had expected.

## What followed

In 1978 the engine grew to 3.3 litres, gained an intercooler and made 300 PS. The 930 stayed in production until 1989, with a four-speed gearbox until its final year. Early cars are known for the pause before the boost arrives and the shove when it does, which is part of their reputation and a reason to treat one with respect on a damp road.

Our [classic 911 guide](/models/911-classic/) covers the 930 alongside the other G-series cars, and [famous Porsche cars](/guides/famous-porsche-cars/) has the 917 and the 935, the racing car that the Turbo became.

## Why it still matters

Most of the current 911 range is turbocharged, and so is every four-cylinder 718. One practical benefit shows up in the mountains. A naturally aspirated engine loses power steadily as the air thins, while a turbocharged one loses far less. On a high road such as the [Million Dollar Highway](/trails/million-dollar-highway/) in Colorado, or the passes of the [Furka, Grimsel and Susten loop](/trails/furka-grimsel-susten-loop/), that is easy to notice.
