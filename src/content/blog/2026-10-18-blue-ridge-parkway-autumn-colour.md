The [Blue Ridge Parkway](/trails/blue-ridge-parkway/) runs 469 miles (755 km) along the crest of the southern Appalachians, from Shenandoah National Park in Virginia to the Great Smoky Mountains in North Carolina. For most of the year it is a fine touring road. In October it is something more, as the ridges turn red, orange and gold and the views stretch for miles.

## Why now

The National Park Service describes autumn as the driest period of the year on the Parkway, with clear skies, cooler weather and only occasional rain. That is also the season's catch: it does not last. The park notes that snow is possible at the higher elevations by November, and that ice and snow close sections of the Parkway in winter. If you want the colour and dry roads together, the next few weeks are the window.

Expect company, especially at the popular overlooks at weekends. A weekday start, early in the morning, gives you the emptiest road and the best light.

## What is closed

The Parkway is not open end to end this autumn. According to the National Park Service road status page, updated on 17 October 2026:

- **Milepost 344.2 to 355.3**, from NC Highway 80 to Mount Mitchell State Park, remains closed because of damage from Hurricane Helene. All recreation in that section is prohibited.
- **Milepost 63.5 to 63.9**, around the James River Bridge in Virginia, is closed for rehabilitation, with a signed detour between mileposts 61.4 and 64.
- The Roanoke Mountain Loop at milepost 120.3 is closed until further notice.

Several facilities are also shut, including the Linn Cove Visitor Center (temporarily), Linville Falls Campground and Crabtree Falls Campground. The status page changes, so check it on the morning you set out.

## Planning the drive

The Mount Mitchell closure splits the most dramatic North Carolina stretch in two. A good plan is to drive the Linn Cove Viaduct and Grandfather Mountain section from the north, leave the Parkway before milepost 344, and rejoin it near Asheville for the high run south past Richland Balsam (6,053 ft) towards Cherokee.

The limit is 45 mph and lower in places, and rangers enforce it. This is a road for an unhurried pace and long stops at the overlooks, which suits a 911 on a grand tour as well as it does a [Taycan](/models/taycan/). There is no fuel on the Parkway itself, so fill up in town every 100 miles or so. Watch for deer at dusk and fog at elevation, both common in autumn.

If you want something tighter afterwards, the [Tail of the Dragon](/trails/tail-of-the-dragon/) is within reach of the southern end. Our [road trip preparation guide](/guides/road-trip-preparation/) covers what to check on the car before a multi-day trip like this one.
