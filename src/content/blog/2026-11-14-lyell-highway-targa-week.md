The [Lyell Highway](/trails/lyell-highway-tasmania/) runs 260 km from Hobart to Queenstown on Tasmania's west coast, and its best months run from October to April. Mid-November is a good time to go: the worst of the winter weather has passed, the days are long, and the summer holiday traffic has not yet arrived. This year it also falls in the week of [Targa Tasmania](/events/), which is worth knowing whether you want to watch the rally or avoid it.

## The road

The first half, through the Derwent Valley past New Norfolk, is fast and open farmland. Beyond Derwent Bridge the highway enters the Franklin–Gordon Wild Rivers National Park, climbs over Mount Arrowsmith on smooth, cambered corners, and then drops into Queenstown down the 99 Bends. That descent, about 6 km of linked corners on a hillside left bare by a century of copper smelting, has long been one of the best-known stages of Targa Tasmania.

Allow around four hours from Hobart, and fill up before you leave the Derwent Valley: fuel stops in the west are far apart.

## Targa Tasmania, 18 to 22 November

Targa Tasmania returned in a revised format in 2025 after a two-year pause, and the 2026 event runs from Wednesday 18 to Sunday 22 November. It starts in Launceston and finishes in Hobart, with the last overnight stop in Burnie on Saturday 21 November. A new three-day sub-event, the Targa Trofeo, runs over the first three days across the north, north-west and east of the island, finishing at Symmons Plains Raceway and back in Launceston on 20 November.

On Sunday 22 November competitors leave Burnie for the West Coast stages before heading to the finish in Hobart, and the Derwent Valley Gazette reports that the rally returns to the Derwent Valley and New Norfolk on the way. So the west of the island is the busy end on Sunday. The organisers publish a summary of road closures on the [Targa website](https://targa.com.au/road-closure/). Check it before you set off on any day of the event week, because closed stages are shut to all other traffic for hours at a time.

## Driving it in a Porsche

Jim Richards and Barry Oliver won Targa Tasmania eight times in Porsches between 1996 and 2006. On the open road, though, the Lyell Highway is one for a steady touring pace: the corners are tight and constant, and traction matters more than power. Plan for:

- **Rain.** The west coast is one of the wettest parts of Australia, and the tarmac under the trees stays damp and mossy all year. A 911 on wide summer tyres needs care on a wet, cold surface. Our [road trip preparation guide](/guides/road-trip-preparation/) covers tyre and fluid checks before a long drive.
- **Wildlife.** Tasmania's animals are on the road at dawn, dusk and after dark. Plan to finish the drive in daylight.
- **Log trucks.** Give them room.
