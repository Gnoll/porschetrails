The high alpine passes have a few weeks left before the snow gates come down. If you want hairpins after that, look south. [Sa Calobra](/trails/sa-calobra/) in Mallorca is open all year, and its best months run from October to May, when the summer heat and much of the summer traffic have gone.

## The road

The MA-2141 was designed in the 1930s by the engineer Antonio Parietti to reach a remote cove on the island's north-west coast without tunnelling. From the Coll dels Reis at 682 m it drops about 680 m to the sea in 13 km, through dozens of bends cut into pale limestone.

The best known is the Nus de sa Corbata, the "Tie Knot", near the top. Here the road loops through 270 degrees and passes under itself on a stone bridge.

## Down, then up

Sa Calobra is a dead end, so every visit is a return trip of about 30 minutes each way. The descent is steep and technical, with rock walls close to the edge of the tarmac. Use a low gear and let the engine do some of the braking. Our piece on [brakes on long descents](/blog/2026-10-03-ceramic-or-iron-brakes-on-long-descents/) explains why that matters.

The climb back is the better half. You can see the next few hairpins stacked above you, which helps you judge what is coming down. The road is narrow for a modern 911, so the pleasure is in placing the car neatly and using its traction out of each slow corner, not in carrying speed.

## When to go

Timing matters more here than on almost any other road in our guide.

- **Go before 9 am or late in the afternoon.** Tour coaches run in convoys from mid-morning and need the whole road on the hairpins. Be ready to stop and give them room.
- **Go now, or in winter.** Hundreds of cyclists climb the road every day from February to May. Autumn is quieter.
- **Go after a dry spell.** Rain brings rock fragments down onto the surface, and goats wander across the road in any weather.

## Getting there

Car ferries sail to Mallorca from Barcelona and Valencia, which makes the island a popular winter destination for sports car owners from across Europe.

Reach Sa Calobra from the MA-10, the main road along the Serra de Tramuntana. It is one of the best drives in Spain in its own right, and the section from Sóller past the Gorg Blau reservoir is especially good.

Before you book the crossing, the [road trip preparation guide](/guides/road-trip-preparation/) covers what to check on the car. If you would rather stay on the mainland, the [N222 in the Douro Valley](/trails/n222-douro-valley/) is also at its best in early autumn, once the grape harvest traffic has cleared.
