The 2026 Porsche Carrera Cup North America season finishes this weekend, 23 to 25 October, at Circuit of the Americas in Austin, Texas. The series runs on the support bill at the United States Grand Prix weekend, so the one-make Porsche field will race in front of one of the biggest crowds of its year. Two classes are already settled. The main Pro title is not.

## Where the championship stands

According to Porsche's report from the penultimate weekend at Road Atlanta, Tyler Maxson of TOPP Racing leads the Pro class by 27 points. He won both races at Road Atlanta on 1 and 2 October, his home track, the first by 8.166 seconds and the second by 5.020 seconds ahead of Aaron Jeansonne.

The finale is a doubleheader, and Porsche puts the points still available at 56. That means Jeansonne can still catch Maxson on paper, but he needs results to go his way in both races. Under the series rules, each race win pays 25 points, with two for pole position and one for fastest lap.

## The titles already won

Two class champions were confirmed at Road Atlanta. Patrick Mulcahy of ACI Motorsports took the Pro-Am title, and marked it by winning the class in the second race. His team-mate Marco Cirone clinched the Masters class with his own second-race win, his first Carrera Cup North America title.

## The car and the circuit

Every driver in the series uses the same car, the 911 Cup, based on the current 992-generation 911. With identical cars, the differences come down to drivers, set-up and tyre management, which is why Carrera Cup has long been a stepping stone towards factory GT seats. If you own a road-going 992, our [911 (992) model guide](/models/911-992/) covers how it relates to the cars on track.

The 5.5 km Austin circuit is listed on our [tracks page](/tracks/). It is known for the steep climb to turn one and the fast sequence of esses that follows, which suits a car with the 911 Cup's traction and braking.
