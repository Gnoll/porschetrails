Cape Town owners hoping for a spring run over [Franschhoek Pass](/trails/franschhoek-pass/) will have to wait a little longer. The pass has been closed to all traffic since May, and the end-of-October reopening the provincial government was working towards has not happened.

## What has changed

In its update of 28 July, the Western Cape Department of Infrastructure said the pass (road MR191) "remains closed until further notice", with a projected "opening under traffic accommodation by end October 2026". It added that weather-related delays had affected progress.

On 27 October, [Bizcommunity reported](https://www.bizcommunity.com/article/western-cape-invests-r1165bn-in-flood-hit-franschhoek-pass-858073a) that the plan is now to reopen the pass during the builder's break, from mid-December to mid-January, under stop/go controls. The report is clear that the pass will stay closed if safety concerns mean traffic cannot get through safely. So the holiday reopening is a target, not a promise.

## Why it is closed

The damage goes back several years. Severe rain in October 2023 caused 16 slope failures along the route. Further heavy rain in May 2026 caused five more slips and eroded the abutment of the Du Toits River bridge on the Villiersdorp side, which is the main reason the whole pass had to shut, including to cyclists and pedestrians.

The repairs are part of a wider R1.165 billion project on MR191. That includes about 16 km between Franschhoek and Theewaterskloof Dam, the stretch that takes in the pass, with resurfacing, new drainage, slope stabilisation using soil nails, steel mesh and gabion walls, and work on the bridge abutment. The whole project is due for completion in July 2027, subject to construction conditions.

## What it means for a drive

Even when the pass reopens, expect stop/go boards and waits, not the smooth, flowing climb the road is known for. Treat any run before the project finishes as a chance to see the road, not to enjoy it at its best. Once the work is done, the new drainage and slope protection should make it less prone to the rockfall closures that have dogged it.

In the meantime the Cape Peninsula still has plenty to offer. [Chapman's Peak Drive](/trails/chapmans-peak-drive/) is twenty minutes from the city, though it also closes at short notice for wind, rain and rockfall, so check the operator's website before you set off. Spring is a good time to have a car looked over before the summer season, and our [road trip preparation guide](/guides/road-trip-preparation/) covers the basics.

Until the department confirms a reopening date, check the Western Cape Government's infrastructure notices before planning a route that relies on the R45 between Franschhoek and Villiersdorp.
