If your Porsche is staying on the road this winter, the most useful job this month is a change of tyres. Here is why, what to buy, and what the law says in Germany and Austria.

## Why summer tyres struggle in the cold

Summer performance tyres use a rubber compound designed to work when it is warm. As the temperature falls, the compound hardens and grips less, on dry roads as well as wet ones. The usual rule of thumb is about 7 °C. Porsche dealers give the same figure when they recommend winter tyres, and Porsche says the rubber in its approved winter tyres does not harden in the cold, so they keep their grip at low temperatures.

This matters more on a Porsche than on most cars. Many models run wide, low-profile summer tyres, and track-biased fitments such as Cup tyres are worse still in the cold. On a rear-engined [911](/models/911-992/), a cold rear tyre that lets go under power or a lift mid-corner is the last thing you want on a damp October morning.

## What the N-marking means

Porsche approves tyres for its cars and marks them with an N on the sidewall, followed by a number (N0, N1, N2 and so on). According to Porsche, approved tyres are tested for handling, braking, wear and rolling resistance, with the construction and compound tailored to Porsche models. The number shows the version of that tyre design: a new evolution gets the next number.

Fit the same tyre, with the same N-marking, on each axle, and choose an N-marked tyre in a size listed for your car. Your handbook or a Porsche Centre can tell you which sizes are approved, and some models need a smaller wheel to take a winter tyre at all.

## The rules in Germany and Austria

Neither country has a fixed date when winter tyres become compulsory for cars. Both use a situational rule instead.

- **Germany:** you must have winter tyres when there is ice, snow, slush or frost on the road. Since 1 October 2024, only tyres with the Alpine symbol (a mountain with a snowflake) count. Tyres marked only M+S no longer qualify. The legal minimum tread is 1.6 mm, though 4 mm is widely recommended for winter use.
- **Austria:** from 1 November to 15 April, cars must have winter tyres on all four wheels in wintry conditions. The minimum tread depth for a winter tyre is 4 mm.

Rules elsewhere differ, so check before you travel.

## A sensible plan

Book the change for a cool week in October, before the first frost rather than after it. Store the summer set cool, dry and out of sunlight. If you are driving south for winter sun, our [road trip preparation guide](/guides/road-trip-preparation/) covers pressures and checks. A cold-weather run on the [Black Forest High Road](/trails/black-forest-high-road/) is much more pleasant on tyres that are working.
