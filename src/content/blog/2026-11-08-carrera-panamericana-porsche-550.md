Every Carrera badge on a Porsche points back to a road race across Mexico that ran only five times. The last of them started 72 years ago this month, on 19 November 1954.

## The race

The Carrera Panamericana was created to celebrate the Mexican section of the Pan-American Highway. It was run on public roads from one end of the country to the other, over several days and through mountains, desert and towns. The 1953 and 1954 editions started in Tuxtla Gutiérrez in the far south and finished at Ciudad Juárez on the US border, eight stages and a little over 3,000 km in all.

It was fast and dangerous, for drivers and for spectators lining the roads. The death toll each year put growing pressure on the Mexican government, and after 1954 the event was not held again in its original form. A revival for historic cars has run since 1988.

## 1953: the first class win

Porsche's first serious attempt came in November 1953. The factory sent two 550 Spyders for Karl Kling and Hans Herrmann, and both retired on the second stage. The class win went instead to a privately entered 550 Coupé driven by the Guatemalans José Herrarte and Carlos González, with the small car finishing 32nd overall.

## 1954: third overall in a 1.5-litre car

A year later, on 19 to 23 November 1954, the result was far bigger. Hans Herrmann finished third overall and won the 1,500 cc sports class in a 550 Spyder, behind only Umberto Maglioli's Ferrari 375 Plus and the Ferrari 375 MM of Phil Hill and Richie Ginther. Jaroslav Juhan was fourth in another 550, 36 seconds behind him on total time. Herrmann went on to give Porsche its first overall Le Mans win in 1970, and you can read more about him in our guide to [famous Porsche drivers](/guides/famous-porsche-drivers/).

A small mid-engined car with a four-cam 1.5-litre flat-four had finished among the big Ferraris on one of the longest and fastest road races in the world. Porsche made sure customers heard about it.

## From race to badge

In 1955 Porsche fitted Ernst Fuhrmann's four-cam engine, developed for the 550, to the road-going [356](/models/356/) and called the result the Carrera. The name stuck to the most powerful versions of the car for the rest of its life.

It moved to the 911 with the Carrera RS 2.7 of 1972, and in time became the name of the standard 911 itself. The [Carrera GT](/models/carrera-gt/) carried it to the top of the range. The 550 that started it all is covered in our [famous Porsche cars](/guides/famous-porsche-cars/) guide.

The same pattern gave us the Targa, named after the Sicilian road race Porsche won eleven times. Both names come from long road races that small, light cars could win against bigger opposition.
