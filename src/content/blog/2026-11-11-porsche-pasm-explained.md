After PDK and PSM, the third set of letters most owners meet is PASM. Here is what it does, plainly.

## What it is

PASM stands for Porsche Active Suspension Management. [Porsche describes it](https://www.porsche.com/stories/innovation/what-is-pasm/) as an electronic damping system that continuously adjusts the dampers to the road and the selected driving mode. It changes how firmly each damper resists movement. It does not change the springs, so it is not the same as the air suspension offered on some larger models.

It arrived with the [997 generation of the 911](/models/911-997/) in 2004. It was standard on the Carrera S and optional on the Carrera, and [Excellence magazine](https://www.excellence-mag.com/of-note/you-ve-got-options-part-i-porsche-active-suspension-management) notes that cars fitted with it sit 10 mm lower than those on the standard chassis. It later spread to the Boxster, Cayman, Cayenne and Panamera, either as standard or as an option.

## How it works

Sensors read steering input, acceleration, braking and the road surface. A control unit uses that information to set the damping force at each of the four dampers separately, adjusting a valve inside each one. Porsche says the changes happen in milliseconds, so the car can be softer on a smooth straight and firmer the moment you turn in or brake hard.

Porsche's technical documentation for the 997 states that if the system develops a fault, the dampers default to their firmest setting, which is the most stable one. A car that suddenly rides very hard, with a warning on the dash, may be telling you exactly that.

## Normal and Sport

On a 997 there is a single button with two settings. Normal is the softer, everyday mode; Sport firms the dampers for less body movement. On a current [992-generation 911](/models/911-992/), damping follows the drive mode (Normal, Sport, Sport Plus, Wet or Individual) and can also be set with its own button marked with a damper symbol, or through PCM.

Firmer is not always better. On a smooth, fast road, Sport keeps the car flatter and more precise. On a bumpy, cambered road such as the [Evo Triangle](/trails/evo-triangle/) in Wales, Normal often lets the wheels follow the surface better and the car feels calmer. Try both on a road you know, within the limits.

## PASM Sport

PASM Sport is a separate option, not a mode. On the 911 it lowers the car by a further 10 mm, with firmer springs, retuned dampers and stiffer anti-roll bars. It is standard on the 911 GTS. It suits owners who drive on smooth roads or circuits, but it is less forgiving on broken surfaces and steep driveways.

## Buying a used car with PASM

The dampers are more expensive to replace than conventional ones, so check for leaks and listen for knocks on a test drive. Our [used 911 and Cayman buying guide](/guides/buying-a-used-911-or-cayman/) covers what else to look for.
