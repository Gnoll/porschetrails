Last week Porsche said it would preview a new mid-engined super sports car platform on 15 October. It has now done so. The car is called the Mission S, and Porsche describes it as a vision model: a design study, not a production car.

## Where and why New York

The Mission S was shown on a pier in Queens, New York, with Manhattan behind it. Porsche chose the spot because the 550 Spyder, which it calls the brand's first super sports car, was photographed there in 1953. The car is the first product shown under the Sportwagenschmiede '35 strategy we covered [last week](/blog/2026-10-08-porsche-strategy-2035-mid-engined-flagship/), and Porsche says it also introduces an evolved design identity for the whole range.

## What Porsche has confirmed

The press release describes the Mission S as representing "a mid-engined super sports car platform positioned above the 911". Porsche intends this to be a permanent part of the line-up rather than a one-off hypercar. That is a change from the [Carrera GT](/models/carrera-gt/) and the [918 Spyder](/models/918-spyder/), each built as a single limited run.

The design borrows openly from earlier cars. Porsche lists round headlights and front wings from the [911](/models/911-classic/), a flat front and a car-in-car rear from the GT1, a roofline that runs to the tail like the 959's, a cooling layout modelled on a radiator fan from the 917, and wheels based on the 928's dial design. There is a fully integrated active rear wing. The show car is finished in Firn White Metallic and Light Red Metallic.

## A cabin without a central screen

The interior is the most unusual part. There is no permanently fitted central display and no fixed passenger screen. Instead, a retractable holder takes a MagSafe-compatible smartphone when you want one. Behind the wheel sit five round instruments, with an analogue needle on the central rev counter. Porsche says the rev counter moves closer to the driver depending on the drive mode, and the dashboard uses new push-pull controls. The layout takes its cue from the 550 Spyder and the 356, and the key holder stays to the left of the steering wheel.

## What has not been said

Porsche has given no engine type, power output, weights, prices or production dates. The release says only that the engine sits in the middle of the car. Several outlets have suggested a new eight-cylinder engine, but Porsche has not confirmed it, so treat that as speculation. The company says it will "soon" carry this direction into its future models.

For owners, the cabin is the clearest signal. Porsche presents the Mission S as a first look at its future design direction, and this one leaves out the large fixed screens found in current models.
