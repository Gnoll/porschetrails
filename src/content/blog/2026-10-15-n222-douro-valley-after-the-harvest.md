As the high Alpine passes close for the winter, the drivers who still want a long road with a view start looking south. One of the easiest answers is northern Portugal, and in particular the [N222 through the Douro Valley](/trails/n222-douro-valley/), which is at its best for the next few weeks.

## Why now

The N222 has two awkward seasons. In July and August the valley floor gets extremely hot, and the river road is no place for a black car with the roof down at three in the afternoon. Then in September and early October comes the grape harvest, when the same road fills with tractors, trailers and lorries moving fruit between the terraces and the wineries.

By mid-October most of that traffic has gone. The days are still mild, the light is lower and softer, and the vines on the terraces start to turn yellow and red. It is one of the two windows (the other is April to June) when the drive is at its best.

## The road itself

The section most people mean is the 27 km between Peso da Régua and Pinhão, on the south bank of the river. It has 93 bends and very little gradient, and in 2015 a study for a car hire company named it the best driving road in the world because of its ratio of corners to straights. Take that with a pinch of salt, but the road is genuinely lovely.

The corners are medium-speed and well sighted, the surface is good, and for almost the whole length you have water on one side and terraces climbing away on the other. It is a third-gear road rather than a road for big speed, and it rewards a smooth, unhurried rhythm far more than effort. A Boxster or a 911 Cabriolet suits it perfectly; see our [718 guide](/models/718-981/) if you are weighing one up.

## Making a day of it

Start early from Régua and you will have the road largely to yourself before the river-cruise coaches reach Pinhão around midday. From Pinhão, the N222 carries on east and climbs away from the river towards São João da Pesqueira, which is quieter and more involving. The N323 and N212 into the hills to the north are tighter again and see very little traffic.

Many of the quintas along the valley offer tastings and lunch, so plan where you will stop and who is driving afterwards. Portugal's drink-drive limit is lower than in England and Wales, and the simplest answer is not to drink at all if you are behind the wheel.

## Before you go

Autumn in the Douro can bring heavy rain, and the surface near the river collects leaves and the odd patch of mud washed down from the terraces. Check the forecast, keep your tyres in good order and treat shaded corners with care in the morning.
