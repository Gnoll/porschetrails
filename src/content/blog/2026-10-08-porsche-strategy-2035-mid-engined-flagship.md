Porsche has set out its plans for the next decade, and there is more in them for sports car owners than the name suggests. The strategy is called Sportwagenschmiede '35 (roughly, "sports car forge") and was presented to investors at a Capital Markets Day this week. Most of it concerns finance and costs. The product section is the part worth reading.

## A sports car above the 911

The headline is a new mid-engined super sports car platform. In Porsche's words, it "will enable a model line that sits above the iconic 911 sports car". Note the phrase "model line": the [Carrera GT](/models/carrera-gt/) and the [918 Spyder](/models/918-spyder/) were each single cars built in limited numbers, with a gap of several years between them.

Porsche has not given a name, a powertrain or a date for a production car. It says it will "provide a preview of a potential mid-engined super sports car platform" on 15 October, so more detail should follow next week.

## The 911 and the 718

The company also says the 911 range "will be strengthened with highly emotive 911 derivatives". It did not say which. The current [992 generation](/models/911-992/) already runs from the Carrera to the GT3 RS and Turbo S, so this points to further special models at the top of the range.

The all-electric 718 Boxster and Cayman remain in the plan. Porsche expects them to support sales "in their first full year of production in 2028". For the background on the mid-engined cars they follow, see our [718 and 981 guide](/models/718-981/).

## Engines, hybrids and batteries

Porsche says it is "sticking to its three-pronged powertrain approach": combustion, plug-in hybrid and electric. It has announced investment in what it calls brand-defining combustion and plug-in hybrid drives, as well as in the next generation of battery technology.

Away from the sports cars, a new SUV in the B segment is due to be presented in 2028 with combustion and plug-in hybrid powertrains, and Porsche is exploring a larger SUV positioned above the Cayenne. By 2030 it plans to launch at least one "brand-defining" new product every year.

## The cost side

The plan comes with a reduction of 9,000 jobs, which Porsche describes as socially responsible and agreed alongside a commitment to secure the jobs of the core workforce until 2035. The company says it will keep to its principle of value over volume.

## What it means for owners

In the short term, nothing changes for the car in your garage. No new model was shown, and the statements above are intentions, not specifications. The next fixed date is the platform preview on 15 October, and we will report what is shown.
