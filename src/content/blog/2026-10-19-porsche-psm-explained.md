Most modern Porsches have a switch or menu option marked PSM. Here is what it does, plainly.

## Where it came from

PSM stands for Porsche Stability Management, Porsche's version of what the wider industry calls electronic stability control. [Ward's described the 1999 model-year 911 Carrera 4](https://www.wardsauto.com/news/archive-wards-1999-porsche-carrera-4/762912/), unveiled in 1998, as Porsche's first car with it, developed with Bosch. That car was part of the water-cooled [996 generation](/models/911-996/), and the system has spread across the range since.

## How it works

PSM constantly compares where you are asking the car to go with where it is actually going. Sensors read each wheel's speed, the steering angle, how fast the car is rotating about its vertical axis (yaw) and how much sideways force it is generating. If the two pictures disagree, the car is starting to slide.

The fix is to brake individual wheels. Ward's noted that the system "can brake any individual wheel to influence the car's dynamic posture". Braking one corner creates a turning force that nudges the car back into line, and PSM can also reduce engine power at the same time. On a low-grip surface under acceleration, the same hardware acts as traction control, braking a spinning wheel or trimming torque so the tyres can grip.

Porsche has always tuned PSM to stay out of the way. Porsche says that with PSM on, the system normally does not intervene until the car reaches the limit of stability.
## PSM Sport and PSM Off

Cars with the Sport Chrono package have long offered a PSM Sport setting. In 2017 [Porsche explained the then-new version](https://newsroom.porsche.com/es/2017/tecnologia/porsche-christophorus-instability-control-sport-mode-16704.html) fitted to the latest 911, 718 and Macan GTS. Rather than simply raising the thresholds, it softens how quickly the car begins to rotate, then gives the driver more room once it judges they are in control. Porsche was clear that it is "not a mode for drifting".

PSM Off is still there, but it is not entirely off. If you brake hard enough to reach the ABS threshold, PSM comes back on to help stabilise the car, a function Porsche says both PSM Sport and PSM Off share.

## What it means for owners

- **Leave it on for the road.** Autumn brings wet leaves, mud from farm traffic and cold tarmac. PSM is a safety net for exactly those surprises.
- **It cannot beat physics.** It works through the tyres, so worn or cold summer tyres limit what it can do. Our [road trip preparation guide](/guides/road-trip-preparation/) covers tyre checks.
- **Save the other modes for the track.** If you want to explore PSM Sport, a circuit day is the place. See our [track day guide](/guides/track-day-modifications/) and the [events calendar](/events/).
- **Warning lights matter.** If a PSM warning stays on, do not assume the safety net is there. Have it checked before a long drive.
