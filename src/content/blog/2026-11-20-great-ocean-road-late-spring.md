The [Great Ocean Road](/trails/great-ocean-road/) runs 243 km along the coast of Victoria, from Torquay past Lorne and Apollo Bay to the Twelve Apostles and on to Allansford. Its best months are usually given as March to May, but late spring has a strong case of its own. The days are long, the Otways are at their greenest, and the summer holiday traffic has not yet arrived.

## Why late November works

Victoria has been on daylight saving since early October, so there is light well into the evening. You can drive the coastal section early, stop for lunch in Apollo Bay, and still reach the Twelve Apostles in good light. Spring weather here changes quickly, so pack a jacket even if the forecast is fine.

From mid-December the road fills with holiday traffic, caravans and coaches, and it stays busy through January. A weekday in the next few weeks is the last quiet chance before the new year.

## The road

The driver's part is the coastal stretch from Anglesea through Lorne to Apollo Bay. Between Lorne and Apollo Bay the road follows every fold of the coast for about 45 km, cut into the cliffs a few metres above the surf. Beyond Apollo Bay it turns inland through the rainforest of the Otway Ranges, one of the wettest parts of the state, then comes back to the coast near the Twelve Apostles.

The road itself is a memorial. It was built between 1919 and 1932, largely by soldiers who had returned from the First World War, in memory of those who did not. The Memorial Arch near Eastern View marks the fact, and it is worth a stop.

For something quieter, Skenes Creek Road, the road from Lorne to Deans Marsh, and Turtons Track wind through the forest with far less traffic.

## Driving it in a Porsche

The speed limit on the coastal section is mostly 80 km/h and lower in places, and it is enforced with cameras. This is a road for the view and the sound of a flat-six with the roof down, which is why open cars such as the [Boxster](/models/718-981/) suit it so well. A few practical points:

- **Tourist drivers.** Many visitors are new to driving on the left, and the road has signs to remind them. Leave room, especially at lookouts and car parks.
- **Wildlife.** Kangaroos and koalas are on the road in the Otways around dusk. Aim to finish the forest sections in daylight.
- **Works and fire season.** The road and its inland routes have had landslip repairs in recent years, so check the Victorian Department of Transport and Planning for current works before you go. As summer approaches, check fire warnings on VicEmergency too.
- **Fuel and tyres.** Fill up in Torquay or Lorne. Our [road trip preparation guide](/guides/road-trip-preparation/) covers the checks worth doing before a long day out.
