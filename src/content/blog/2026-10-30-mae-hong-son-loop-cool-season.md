The [Mae Hong Son Loop](/trails/mae-hong-son-loop/) is a 600 km circuit from Chiang Mai through Pai, Mae Hong Son and Mae Sariang, and its season is the cool, dry months from November to February. In a normal year the season turns in October. This year it is running late.

## A late start to the season

On 15 October the Thai Meteorological Department forecast that upper Thailand would enter the cool season in the third week of October, about a week later than usual, and that it would last until around mid-February 2027. That has not happened yet. On 28 October the department said morning temperatures had not fallen far enough to declare the start of winter, and that conditions were likely to stay variable into early November.

The department only declares the season when three things settle: minimum temperatures below 23 °C across a wide area, low-level winds turning to blow mainly from the north-east or east, and a steady fall in rain. Its outlook also expects a milder winter than last year, with El Niño strengthening towards the end of 2026. The coolest weeks are forecast for late December to early February, with frost possible on the highest ground.

For drivers, the practical point is simple. Early November may still bring afternoon storms and wet hairpins, so if your dates are flexible, aim for mid-November onwards, and plan to finish well before the smoky burning season that usually arrives from March.

## The drive

Go anticlockwise. Route 1095 from Chiang Mai to Pai is 130 km of almost continuous second- and third-gear bends over forested ridges, and it carries on in the same way to Mae Hong Son. The road is advertised as having 1,864 curves, and the provincial chamber of commerce will sell you a certificate to prove you drove them. The southern half on Route 108 through Mae Sariang is faster and more open, and a detour up Doi Inthanon takes you to Thailand's highest point.

Allow three to four days. This is a touring road, not a place to hurry: minibuses and pickups cut corners on 1095, scooters crowd the roads around Pai, and traffic drives on the left.

## In your Porsche

The surface on Route 1095 is mostly good but has sudden rough patches, steep cambers and diesel spills on the hairpins. A Carrera or a [Boxster or Cayman](/models/718-981/) on standard suspension suits it better than a low GT car.

Fuel needs a little planning. Porsche engines are designed for 98 RON and will run on 95 with reduced performance, but the best grades are not available everywhere, so fill up in the larger towns.
