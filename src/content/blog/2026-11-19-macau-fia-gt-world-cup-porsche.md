The last big GT3 race of the year takes place this weekend on the streets of Macau. The FIA GT World Cup runs as part of the 73rd Macau Grand Prix from 19 to 22 November, and Porsche has the largest single share of the grid ([Pit Debrief](https://www.pitdebrief.com/post/eight-manufacturers-and-18-drivers-confirmed-for-2026-macau-fia-gt-world-cup/)).

## The Porsche line-up

Four Porsche 911 GT3 R (992) Evo cars are entered, supported by Porsche Motorsport Asia Pacific and run under the Absolute Racing banner ([Dailysportscar](https://www.dailysportscar.com/2026/11/19/fia-gt-world-cup.html)):

- #8 Laurin Heinrich (AO by Absolute)
- #618 Dorian Boccolacci (Absolute Racing)
- #911 Alessio Picariello (Tempo by Absolute Racing)
- #992 Thomas Preining (Tempo by Absolute Racing)

Preining arrives as this year's DTM champion, having taken his second title at Hockenheim in October ([Porsche Newsroom](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/ppdb/2026/10/porsche-factory-driver-thomas-preining-wins-the-dtm-drivers-championship.html)). Heinrich finished third in Macau last year, when Antonio Fuoco won both races for Ferrari ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_FIA_GT_World_Cup)). He made a good start this time, setting the fastest time in the first practice session on Thursday ahead of Raffaele Marciello and Maro Engel ([Pit Debrief](https://www.pitdebrief.com/post/2026-macau-gp-fia-gt-world-cup-fp1-report-and-results/)).

The car is the customer racing version of the [992-generation 911](/models/911-992/), the same basic machine that Manthey raced to a class win at Monza earlier this month.

## A gap in the record

For all its success in GT3 racing around the world, Porsche has not yet won the FIA GT World Cup. This is the ninth running of the event, and the grid of 18 cars from eight manufacturers is a record for the FIA GT World Cup era. Mercedes-AMG and BMW have three cars each, Ferrari and Audi two each, Aston Martin returns after 11 years with two Vantages, and Lamborghini and McLaren have one car apiece.

The race is also the second edition to use torque sensors on the cars to measure performance, part of how the organisers balance such a mixed field.

## How the weekend runs

All times are local to Macau:

- **Friday:** two qualifying sessions, from 15:50 to 16:20 and 16:40 to 17:05
- **Saturday:** a 12-lap qualification race at 14:35, which sets the grid for Sunday
- **Sunday:** the 16-lap FIA GT World Cup race at 12:35

Every session is shown live and free on YouTube. The Guia circuit is narrow and walled, with very little room for error, so a clean Saturday matters as much as outright pace.
