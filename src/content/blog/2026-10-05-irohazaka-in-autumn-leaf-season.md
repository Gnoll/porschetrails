The next few weeks are the most beautiful time of year on [Irohazaka](/trails/irohazaka/), and the worst time to drive it without a plan. The maples and beeches on the mountainside above Nikkō usually turn from mid to late October, and the wider area stays busy with leaf watchers into early November.

## The road

Irohazaka is two one-way roads between Nikkō and Lake Chūzenji. The second Irohazaka carries traffic uphill on two lanes and 20 corners. The first Irohazaka brings you back down through 28 tighter hairpins. Each of the 48 bends is signed with a character from the old Japanese "iroha" syllabary, which is where the name comes from.

Because you never meet oncoming traffic, it is an easy road to place a wide car on. Lake Chūzenji at the top sits at 1,269 m, so the colour arrives there first and works its way down the hill over a couple of weeks.

## The problem in October

Japanese tourism sources put the uphill drive at about 20 minutes in normal conditions and two to three hours at the height of the leaf season. Weekdays are better than weekends, but tour coaches run all week.

A queue like that is no fun in any car, and it is harder work in one with a manual gearbox and a firm clutch. A PDK car or a [Taycan](/models/taycan/) is the calmer choice if you have one.

## How to time it

- **Go early.** Be at the foot of the climb soon after sunrise. The queue builds through the morning as coaches arrive from Tokyo.
- **Go on a weekday.** Avoid Saturdays, Sundays and public holidays if you can.
- **Stay at the top.** A night beside Lake Chūzenji lets you drive up late the previous afternoon and come down before the crowds arrive.
- **Start with a full tank or battery.** You may spend a long time moving at walking pace.

## At the top and on the way down

The uphill road ends near Akechidaira, where a ropeway climbs to a viewpoint over Kegon Falls and the lake. The car park there fills early in the season.

The descent is steep and hard on brakes, more so in slow traffic where you are on the pedal constantly. Use a low gear and let the engine hold the car. Our piece on [brakes on long descents](/blog/2026-10-03-ceramic-or-iron-brakes-on-long-descents/) explains why.

Wild monkeys sit on the walls and the road and have learned that cars carry food. Keep the windows closed and do not feed them.

## If you would rather have an empty road

Come back in late spring, when the hill is green and quiet, or combine a Nikkō trip with the [Hakone Turnpike](/trails/hakone-turnpike/) south-west of Tokyo, a toll road with far fewer coaches. The [road trip preparation guide](/guides/road-trip-preparation/) covers what to check before either.
